Reading the letters on the yantra

Every character and combination you are likely to meet on the copper plate, with IAST transliteration. Companion to The Śrī Yantra, Explained.

1 · How Devanagari works

Devanagari is a syllabic script. Each consonant letter carries a built-in inherent vowel “a”: क alone reads ka, not k. Other vowels replace the inherent one via small satellite signs (mātrās) attached to the consonant: का kā, कि ki, कु ku. A subscript stroke ् (virāma) deletes the vowel entirely: क् is bare k. When two or more consonants meet with no vowel between them, they fuse into a single stacked or blended shape — a conjunct — which is why mantric syllables can look so intricate. The horizontal headline joins the letters of a word; on an engraving it is often the clearest guide to where words begin and end.

2 · The sixteen vowels — the sixteen petals

The independent vowel letters are used at the start of a syllable; the mātrā column shows the same vowel as it attaches to a consonant (shown on क k). The Śrī Vidyā tradition counts exactly sixteen — they are engraved one per petal on the outer sixteen-petal lotus of this plate, each paired with its ākarṣiṇī goddess.

The last two are strictly vowel a + a mark (anusvāra / visarga), but the mātṛkā ("alphabet-goddess") tradition counts them as vowels 15 and 16.

3 · The consonants

Arranged in the traditional grid: five rows by place of articulation (each row runs unvoiced, unvoiced-aspirate, voiced, voiced-aspirate, nasal), then semivowels and sibilants. Every glyph below reads with the inherent a.

5 · Numerals

The engraver numbered many of the deity names on this plate (e.g. १ कामेश्वरी…), so the numerals are load-bearing: the number often identifies a ring position even when the name is worn.

6 · The bīja syllables on this plate

Seed syllables — single compressed utterances treated as the sound-body of a deity. These are the ones engraved on (or associated with) this yantra.

7 · Conjuncts you will meet on the plate

The fused consonant clusters, each broken into its parts, with a word from this plate where it occurs. Two recurring special forms: र as a first member becomes a small hook (repha) written above the following letter — सर्व sarva hides its r up top; र as a second member becomes a diagonal stroke below — क्र kra, प्र pra.
